Led the development of the company's first automated smart surveillance system for a global investment organization, creating an event-driven computer vision platform capable of operating autonomously over visual information.

The system combined person tracking, face detection and recognition, office-space recognition, activity-area detection, and suspicious-activity detection to understand how individuals interacted with physical spaces. Visual inference was used to create unique customer profiles and derive activity insights that could improve customer support and situational awareness.

A central challenge was moving beyond isolated computer vision predictions. Detecting a face, person, or location provides only a momentary observation; meaningful intelligence emerges when those observations can be associated across time and transformed into events and persistent contextual information.

The project established much of my practical foundation in computer vision and helped shape how I approach AI systems today: not simply as individual models, but as coordinated pipelines where perception, state, events, and business logic work together to solve an end-to-end problem.
